Glass specification

6 mm, 10 mm or laminated? Choosing glass for a Nigerian home

Thickness is only one part of the decision. The opening, support, exposure, safety and comfort requirements all matter.

18 August 2026 · Oteh Miroko Aluminum

When comparing window quotations, it is tempting to choose by glass thickness alone. A safe, comfortable installation depends on more than a number: the pane size, how it is supported, the frame system, where the window sits and how people will use the space all affect the specification.

Start with the opening and frame

Large panes, exposed elevations and different frame designs place different demands on the glass and its supports. The aluminium system and the glass should be selected together. Share the opening measurements and any drawings with your fabricator so the proposed combination can be checked for the actual conditions.

A thickness such as 6 mm or 10 mm is not a universal recommendation. The appropriate choice depends on the pane dimensions, edge support and project requirements; have the final specification confirmed by a qualified glazing professional.

Consider safety and location

For doors, low-level glazing, stair areas and other places where people may come into contact with glass, ask whether a safety-glazing option is required. Toughened and laminated glass behave differently when damaged. Laminated glass uses an interlayer that can help retain fragments, but it is not automatically suitable for every security or impact requirement.

Confirm the appropriate safety class and installation details for the location rather than relying on the product name alone.

Think about heat, light and privacy

Clear, tinted, reflective and Low-E options affect daylight, privacy and solar heat in different ways. The best fit depends on window orientation, shading, room use and the appearance you want. Ask for samples and compare them in the kind of light the room receives before settling on a finish.

What to ask for in a quotation

  • The glass type and nominal thickness for each opening.
  • Whether safety glazing is specified where people may impact the glass.
  • The frame system and how the pane is supported and sealed.
  • Any tint, coating or privacy option, with a physical sample if available.

A clear, opening-by-opening specification makes quotations easier to compare and helps ensure the selected glass suits the finished installation.
