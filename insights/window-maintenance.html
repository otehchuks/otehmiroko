Harmattan checks: a 20-minute routine for sliding windows

Dust can collect in tracks and drainage slots. A gentle clean and a quick check of the moving parts helps you spot issues early.

2 July 2026 · Oteh Miroko Aluminum

Sliding windows work best when their tracks, drainage paths and rollers are free of grit. During dusty weather, spend a few minutes checking them rather than forcing a sash that has started to stick. The routine below uses simple cleaning and observation; follow the window-system manufacturer's instructions for any adjustment or lubrication.

A four-step seasonal check

  1. Remove loose dust. Open the sash if it moves freely, then use a soft brush or vacuum attachment to lift grit from the visible track and corners.
  2. Wipe the track gently. Use a soft cloth and mild soapy water, then dry the area. Avoid abrasive pads, harsh chemicals and excess water around drainage openings.
  3. Check the drainage slots. Make sure the small weep openings are clear. Remove loose debris carefully; do not seal or enlarge the openings.
  4. Test the movement and seals. Slide the sash without forcing it. Look for uneven movement, damaged weather seals, loose handles or unusual scraping sounds.

What not to do

Do not force a jammed panel, dismantle a heavy sash or apply oil or grease without checking the system guidance; some lubricants attract more dust. Avoid leaning out of a window or using a ladder to reach high openings. Ask a window technician to inspect rollers, alignment or hardware that needs adjustment.

When to call for service

Arrange a professional check if the frame is visibly out of square, the glass or seal is damaged, water is entering the room, or the sash still binds after cleaning. A small maintenance visit can identify whether the cause is debris, worn rollers, an alignment issue or a damaged component.

For routine care, repeat the check after especially dusty periods and at least seasonally. Keep a note of any change in movement so you can describe it clearly when requesting service.
